## 3 Things That Matter More Than Price

### 1. Inverter Wattage Over Capacity

Most people compare Wh and ignore inverter wattage. That is backwards. Wh tells you how long it runs. Inverter wattage tells you what it can start. A chest freezer surges to 2–3x its running wattage at startup. A 1,000W inverter fails that test. A 2,000W inverter passes it.

**Minimum for home backup:** 1,500W. Recommended: 2,000W.

For a full breakdown of surge numbers by appliance type see the [surge vs running watts guide](/blog/best-solar-generator-chest-freezer-2026/).

### 2. LiFePO4 Battery Chemistry Only

Every generator on this list uses LiFePO4. It lasts 3,000–3,500 cycles vs 500 for older lithium-ion. At one full cycle per week that is 57 years of theoretical battery life. Do not buy any sub-$1000 generator that does not specify LiFePO4 — several budget brands still use NMC chemistry without disclosing it clearly.

### 3. Solar Input Capacity

For outages longer than one day, solar recharge speed is the deciding factor. A generator that takes 8 hours to recharge from solar is nearly useless during a 72-hour grid failure with limited sun.

**Minimum:** 200W solar input. Recommended for home backup: 400W+.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the best solar generator under $1000 for home backup?**
The Jackery Explorer 1000 V2 is the best overall pick under $1000. It has a 2,000W inverter, 1,070Wh LiFePO4 battery, and 14+ hours of chest freezer runtime per charge. The EcoFlow DELTA 3 Plus wins if fast solar recharging is your priority.

**Can a solar generator under $1000 run a refrigerator?**
Yes. Both the Jackery Explorer 1000 V2 and EcoFlow DELTA 3 Plus run a standard 18 cubic foot refrigerator for 10–12 hours per charge. The key is inverter surge capacity — both units handle the compressor startup surge that kills cheaper generators.

**Is 1000Wh enough for home backup power?**
For essential loads — refrigerator, chest freezer, router, phone charging, and LED lights — 1,000Wh provides 8–14 hours of backup depending on usage. For 24-hour coverage plan on either a larger unit or a solar panel to recharge during the day.

**What is the difference between Wh and W in a solar generator?**
Wh (watt-hours) is capacity — how much total energy the battery holds. W (watts) is inverter power — the maximum load it can handle at once. Both numbers matter. High Wh with a low-watt inverter will fail to start your refrigerator compressor even if it has plenty of stored energy.

**How does the Jackery 1000 V2 compare to the EcoFlow DELTA 3 Plus?**
The Jackery has a higher 2,000W inverter and costs less at ~$799. The EcoFlow charges faster — 58 minutes from 800W solar vs 2+ hours for the Jackery. For short outages the Jackery wins on value. For multi-day outages the EcoFlow wins on recharge speed.

**How long does a solar generator battery last?**
LiFePO4 solar generators like the Jackery 1000 V2 and EcoFlow DELTA 3 Plus are rated for 3,000–3,500 cycles. At one full cycle per week that equals 57–67 years of theoretical battery life.
