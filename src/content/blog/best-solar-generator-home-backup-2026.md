## ⚡ Why Most Backup Generators Fail When You Need Them Most

Before I get to the winners, let me tell you the mistake almost everyone makes.

They buy a generator based on the running watts number on the box. They see 1,000W and think that means their 800W freezer is covered. It is not.

Every compressor-based appliance - freezer, refrigerator, air conditioner - has two power numbers: running watts and surge watts. The surge happens the moment the compressor kicks on. For a typical chest freezer, that surge can be 3x the running watts, lasting about half a second.

If your generator cannot deliver that surge, the unit trips. In a real blackout, that means your food is gone by morning.

I wrote a full breakdown on [how to calculate surge watts for your freezer here](/blog/what-is-lra-on-a-freezer/). Read that first if you are not sure what your freezer needs. It could save you from buying the wrong unit.

## 🏆 The 3 Best Solar Generators for Home Backup in 2026

### 1. EcoFlow DELTA 3 Plus - Best Overall 🥇

The EcoFlow DELTA 3 Plus is the strongest all-round unit of the three for home backup.

The DELTA 3 Plus has a 1,024Wh battery, 1,800W of continuous AC output and a published 3,600W surge. Its X-Boost feature can run resistive loads up to 2,200W on US models, and the high surge rating is what lets it start a chest freezer compressor.

On the figures in our [chest freezer wattage chart](/blog/how-many-watts-chest-freezer/), here is what to expect:

- ⚡ **Running watts on a typical chest freezer:** about 85-110W while the compressor runs (about 45W averaged over a day)
- ✅ **Startup surge:** a typical chest freezer needs roughly 600-1,200W, well inside the unit's 3,600W surge rating
- 🕐 **Calculated runtime on a full charge:** about 19 hours for a typical freezer (15% battery buffer), or about 10 hours with a refrigerator added
- ☀️ **Solar recharge time:** about 3.4 hours from 400W of panels at 75% real-world output (calculated)

It also charges from zero to 100% in 56 minutes from a wall outlet. That matters because the grid often returns within a day or two, and you want the unit full again quickly.

The EcoFlow app shows real-time watt draw, battery percentage and estimated runtime on your phone, which is useful when the unit sits in a garage.

**Trade-offs:** it has half the capacity of the Bluetti, and it typically costs more per watt-hour.

**Best for:** Homeowners who want a single unit that can handle a chest freezer, refrigerator, router, and lights simultaneously during an outage.

### 2. Jackery Explorer 1000 V2 - Best Mid-Range Pick 🥈

If the EcoFlow is out of your budget, the Jackery Explorer 1000 V2 is the one to look at next.

It has a 1,070Wh battery, 1,500W of continuous AC output and a published 3,000W surge. That clears the startup surge of every common chest freezer size, but its continuous output leaves less room than the EcoFlow for adding other appliances.

So before you buy, [check your freezer's LRA number](/blog/what-is-lra-on-a-freezer/). Multiply it by 120 to get your startup surge. Anything under about 2,500W is comfortably within the Jackery's 3,000W surge rating.

What the numbers show:

- **Running watts:** about 85-110W on a typical chest freezer while the compressor runs
- ✅ **Startup surge:** 3,000W surge rating, above the roughly 600-1,200W a typical freezer needs
- **Calculated runtime on a full charge:** about 20 hours for a typical freezer (15% battery buffer)
- **Solar recharge:** about 3.6 hours from 400W of panels at 75% real-world output (calculated)

**Best for:** Budget-conscious homeowners with standard LRA freezers who want reliable backup at a lower price point.

### 3. Bluetti Elite 200 V2 - Best for Extended Backup 🥉

This one is in a different category. The Elite 200 V2 has a 2,073.6Wh battery and 2,600W of continuous output. Bluetti does not publish a motor-start surge figure for it, but says it handles resistive loads up to 3,900W.

On the calculated figures, a chest freezer, a refrigerator, a few LED lights and a router (about 90-125W combined average) run about 14 to 20 hours on a single charge.

- **Combined average draw (freezer, fridge, lights, router):** about 90-125W
- **Calculated runtime on a full charge:** about 14-20 hours (15% battery buffer)
- **Solar recharge:** about 6.9 hours from 400W of panels at 75% real-world output (calculated), with up to 1,000W of solar input

**Best for:** Homeowners who want longer backup for several appliances and are willing to carry a heavier unit (53.4 lb).

## ❓ FAQ

**What size solar generator do I need for home backup?**
For a single chest freezer or refrigerator, a 1,000-1,200Wh unit with a surge rating above your appliance's startup surge handles most situations. If you want to run multiple appliances simultaneously, go with 2,000Wh or more.

**Can a solar generator power a whole house?**
Not a standard portable unit. Whole-home backup requires a permanently installed battery system like a Tesla Powerwall. Portable solar generators are designed for essential appliances during an outage.

**How long does a solar generator last on one charge?**
It depends entirely on what you are running. A typical chest freezer averages about 45W over a day, so a 1,024Wh battery runs it for roughly 19 hours (15% battery buffer). Add a refrigerator and lights and you are looking at about 10 hours.
