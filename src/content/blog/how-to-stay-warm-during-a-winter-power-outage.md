In a winter outage the goal is simple: **keep heat on your body and in one small room**, and never use anything that produces carbon monoxide indoors. Battery power helps, but only with the right loads.

## 🔥 First: Carbon Monoxide Rules

The CDC is clear:

- **Never use a generator inside your home or garage**, even with doors and windows open. Run it more than 20 feet from windows, doors and vents.
- **Never use a gas stove, oven, charcoal grill or portable camp stove to heat your home.**
- **Install battery-powered CO alarms** near every sleeping area.
- Symptoms include headache, dizziness, weakness, nausea and confusion. Sleeping people can die before symptoms appear.

## 🧥 Keep Warm Without Power

1. **Layer clothing**, including hats and socks.
2. **Close off one room** and live in it. Hang blankets over doors and windows.
3. **Use sleeping bags and blankets.** Several layers beat one thick one.
4. **Stay dry** and keep moving gently. Eat and drink regularly.
5. **Keep the garage and unused rooms closed** to hold heat in.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I stay warm in a power outage without heat?
Layer clothes, stay in one small room with doors and windows covered, use blankets and sleeping bags, and stay dry. Use an electric blanket if you have battery power.

### Is it safe to use a gas oven for heat?
No. The CDC warns that gas stoves, ovens, grills and camp stoves produce carbon monoxide and should never be used to heat a home.

### How far from the house should a generator be?
The CDC says more than 20 feet from windows, doors and vents, and never inside the home or garage.

### How long will a battery run an electric blanket?
At 100W, roughly 9 hours on a 1,000Wh class power station; at 200W, roughly 4 to 5 hours (calculated using 85% usable capacity).

### What are the signs of carbon monoxide poisoning?
Headache, dizziness, weakness, upset stomach, vomiting, chest pain and confusion. Sleeping or intoxicated people can die before symptoms appear.
