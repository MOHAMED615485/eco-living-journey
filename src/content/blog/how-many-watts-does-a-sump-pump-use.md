A typical home sump pump draws roughly **400 to 1,500 watts while running** and needs a startup surge of **about 800 to 4,500 watts**, depending on horsepower. The good news is that a pump rarely runs continuously, so its energy use is driven by how hard it is raining.

<p style="font-size:0.85rem;color:#555;"><em>How the numbers work: wattages are typical ranges from manufacturer spec sheets and efficiency labels, so check the label on your own appliance. Runtimes are calculated, not lab-tested: usable energy = rated Wh &times; 0.85, divided by average watts.</em></p>


## ⚡ Sump Pump Wattage by Horsepower

These ranges are typical figures published in power-station makers' sump pump guides. They are a planning guide, so confirm the actual amps on your pump's label.

| Pump size | Running watts | Startup (surge) watts |
|:--|:--:|:--:|
| 1/4 HP | 400-600W | 800-1,800W |
| 1/3 HP | 600-800W | 1,200-2,400W |
| 1/2 HP | 800-1,000W | 2,400-3,000W |
| 3/4 HP | 1,000-1,500W | 3,000-4,500W |

## 🌧️ How Long Does the Pump Actually Run?

Pump run time depends on how fast water enters the pit. Typical examples used in sizing guides:

- **Light rain:** about 5 minutes per hour.
- **Heavy rain:** about 20 minutes per hour.

For a 750W pump (a 1/3 to 1/2 HP class pump), that works out to roughly **62.5Wh per hour in light rain** and **250Wh per hour in heavy rain**.

**How to read it:** a 1,000Wh class unit only runs a 750W pump for about an hour *continuously*, but it can cover the pump for **3 to 4 hours in heavy rain** and more than half a day in light rain. A multi-hour storm is why capacity matters more than output rating here.

## ⚠️ Surge and Safety

- **Surge:** a 1/2 HP pump can need 2,400-3,000W at startup. The DELTA 3 Plus (3,600W surge) and Jackery Explorer 1000 V2 (3,000W surge) cover that range on paper. Bluetti does not publish a motor surge figure we can verify for the Elite 200 V2, so check before relying on it for a large pump.
- **Plug into the unit directly.** Do not wire a power station into your home's electrical panel without a licensed electrician and a proper transfer switch. Never backfeed a panel.
- **Consider a dedicated backup pump.** A battery-backed DC backup sump pump is built for exactly this job, and a portable power station is a flexible second layer, not a replacement for a proper backup system if you live in a flood-prone area.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

### How many watts does a sump pump use?
Typically about 400-1,500 running watts depending on horsepower, with a startup surge of about 800-4,500 watts. The label on your pump gives the real amps; multiply by 120 volts.

### Can a portable power station run a sump pump?
Yes, if its surge rating covers the pump's startup spike and it has enough watt-hours for the storm. A 1,000Wh class unit runs a 750W pump for about an hour continuously, or a few hours in heavy rain when the pump cycles.

### How long does a sump pump run per hour?
Examples used in sizing guides are about 5 minutes per hour in light rain and about 20 minutes per hour in heavy rain, but your pit and groundwater conditions can differ.

### Is a battery backup sump pump better than a power station?
A dedicated battery backup pump is purpose-built and automatic. A power station is flexible and can also run other loads, but it needs the pump to be plugged into it and does not switch over automatically.

### Can I connect a power station to my home electrical panel?
Not directly. Panel connections should be done by a licensed electrician with a proper transfer switch. Never backfeed a panel through an outlet.
