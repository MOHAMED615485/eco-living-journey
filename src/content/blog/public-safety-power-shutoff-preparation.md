**A Public Safety Power Shutoff (PSPS) is a planned outage.** Utilities in wildfire-prone areas, including PG&E in California, may shut power off on purpose during dangerous weather to reduce the risk of their equipment starting a fire.

## ⚡ Quick Answer

- **PSPS is deliberate**, triggered by high winds, dry conditions and fire risk, not a random failure.
- **PG&E says its goal is 1 to 2 days of notice** where possible, but notice can be shorter. Sign up for alerts and keep contact details current.
- **Medical Baseline and Vulnerable Customer programs** exist for people who rely on powered medical equipment. Ask your utility about eligibility.
- **Dial 211** for local resources and information during an emergency.
- **Plan your own backup power** for medical devices, communications, lights and food.

## 📣 Step 1: Get Alerts

Register your phone and email with your utility, and keep the number up to date. Check your address on the utility's PSPS map during fire season. Charge phones, power banks and your power station when a warning is issued.

## 🏥 Step 2: Tell Your Utility About Medical Needs

If someone in the home relies on electricity for medical equipment, ask your utility about its medical or vulnerable customer programs. These programs are designed to help with notification, and they do not replace your own backup power plan. See our guides on the [best solar generator for medical equipment](/blog/best-solar-generator-medical-equipment/) and [CPAP machines](/blog/best-solar-generator-cpap-machine/).

## 🔋 Step 3: Build Your Backup Plan

Utility notices typically do not state how long the shutoff will last, so plan for a multi-day outage. A reasonable minimum kit:

1. Phone charging and a router or hotspot.
2. Lights (see [candles vs flashlights](/blog/candles-vs-flashlights-power-outage/)).
3. Fridge and freezer plan (see [can you refreeze food](/blog/can-you-refreeze-food-after-a-power-outage/)).
4. Medical device power, if needed.
5. Water and food for several days. See [emergency water storage](/blog/emergency-water-storage/).

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a Public Safety Power Shutoff?
A planned outage by a utility in a wildfire-prone area during dangerous weather, done to reduce the risk of equipment starting a fire.

### How much notice will I get?
PG&E says its goal is 1 to 2 days of notice where possible, but conditions can change and notice may be shorter.

### How long does a PSPS last?
Utility notices do not guarantee a duration, so plan for multiple days and check your utility's updates.

### Can I register for medical needs?
Utilities such as PG&E offer Medical Baseline and Vulnerable Customer programs. Contact your utility for eligibility, and keep your own backup power.

### What should I charge before a shutoff?
Phones, power banks, your power station, flashlights and any medical device batteries.
