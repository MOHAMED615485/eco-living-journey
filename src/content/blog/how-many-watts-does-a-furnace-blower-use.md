A gas furnace makes heat with gas, but it needs **electricity to run the blower fan and the controls**. In a winter outage the furnace usually will not run at all, which is why people ask whether a power station can run it. The answer depends on the blower motor type: **about 60-300W for efficient ECM motors, and 450-900W for older PSC motors.**

<p style="font-size:0.85rem;color:#555;"><em>How the numbers work: wattages are typical ranges from manufacturer spec sheets and efficiency labels, so check the label on your own appliance. Runtimes are calculated, not lab-tested: usable energy = rated Wh &times; 0.85, divided by average watts.</em></p>


## ⚡ Blower Motor Watts by Type

The blower uses more than 90% of a furnace's electricity. Typical ranges (for a furnace around 80,000 BTU, fan set to normal operation):

| Blower motor type | Typical running watts |
|:--|:--:|
| PSC single-speed | 500-900W |
| PSC multi-speed | 450-800W |
| ECM, single-stage furnace | 250-300W |
| ECM, two-stage or modulating | 90-200W (can reach 600W+) |
| ECM at its lowest speed | as low as 60W |

The only certain way to know your furnace is to read the rating plate or measure it with a plug-in watt meter. Igniter and surge figures are not covered by these ranges, so check the manufacturer's documentation.

## 📊 Calculated Hours of Heat

A furnace does not run constantly. Assuming it runs about **half the time on a cold night (a 50% duty cycle)**:

| Furnace type | EcoFlow DELTA 3 Plus | Jackery Explorer 1000 V2 | Bluetti Elite 200 V2 | EcoFlow DELTA Pro | Bluetti EB3A |
|:--|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| Efficient ECM blower, 250W while running (about 125W average) | 7.0 h | 7.3 h | 14.1 h | 24.5 h | 1.8 h |
| Older PSC blower, 700W while running (about 350W average) | 2.5 h | 2.6 h | 5.0 h | 8.7 h | 0.7 h |


**What this means:** an efficient ECM furnace can be kept going for roughly a night on a 1,000Wh class unit and longer on a 2,000Wh class unit. An older PSC furnace drains the same battery in about 2.5 hours. Real duty cycles depend on outdoor temperature and your home's insulation.

## ⚠️ Safety First

- **Plug-in or hardwired?** Most furnaces are hardwired. Connecting a hardwired furnace to a power station needs a licensed electrician and an approved transfer arrangement. Never backfeed your panel through an outlet.
- **Follow the manufacturer's instructions** and local code. If you are unsure, call a licensed HVAC technician.
- **Carbon monoxide:** the CDC says never run a generator inside your home or garage, and to place any fuel-burning generator more than 20 feet from windows, doors and vents. Install battery-powered CO alarms near sleeping areas. A battery power station produces no exhaust, but your gas furnace and any other fuel-burning heater still do.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

### Does a gas furnace need electricity?
Yes. The gas makes the heat, but the blower fan, igniter and control board need electricity, so most gas furnaces will not run during a power outage.

### How many watts does a furnace blower use?
Roughly 60-300W for efficient ECM motors and 450-900W for older PSC motors, according to HVAC reference ranges. Check your furnace's rating plate or measure it with a watt meter.

### Can a portable power station run a furnace?
Often yes for the blower itself, if it is wired or plugged in safely and the unit's output and surge rating are high enough. Hardwired furnaces need a licensed electrician; do not backfeed your panel.

### How long will a power station run a furnace?
Assuming about half-time operation, a 250W ECM blower averages roughly 125W, so a 1,000Wh class unit gives roughly 7 hours. A 700W PSC blower averages about 350W and gives roughly 2.5 hours.

### Is it safe to run a furnace on a generator?
Only if installed and connected according to the manufacturer's instructions and local code. Never run a fuel-burning generator indoors or in a garage, and keep it more than 20 feet from windows, doors and vents.
