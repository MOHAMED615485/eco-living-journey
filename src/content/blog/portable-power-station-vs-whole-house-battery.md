A portable power station and a whole-house battery solve different problems. A **portable power station** keeps your *critical loads* (fridge, router, lights, medical devices) running and plugs in with a normal cord. A **whole-house battery** is installed by an electrician and can run most of the home's circuits.

<p style="font-size:0.85rem;color:#555;"><em>How the numbers work: wattages are typical ranges from manufacturer spec sheets and efficiency labels, so check the label on your own appliance. Runtimes are calculated, not lab-tested: usable energy = rated Wh &times; 0.85, divided by average watts.</em></p>


## 📏 Scale: Why the Difference Is Big

According to the U.S. Energy Information Administration, the average American home uses roughly **10,800 kWh per year (10,791 kWh in 2022), or about 29 kWh per day**. A 2,073.6Wh (2.07kWh) unit like the Bluetti Elite 200 V2 holds about **1.7 hours of average whole-house use**. That is why portable units are for selected appliances, not for the entire house.

| | Portable power station | Whole-house battery |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Typical capacity | about 0.3-4 kWh | about 10 kWh and up |
| Installation | none, plug in | licensed electrician, transfer switch or integrated panel |
| What it powers | chosen appliances | most or all circuits |
| Portable | yes | no |
| Cost | far lower | several times higher plus installation |
| Best for | fridge, router, lights, CPAP, power tools | whole-home backup, solar-plus-storage |

## 🧮 Pick by Load, Not Hype

1. **List the loads you must keep running** (fridge, freezer, router, lights, medical device).
2. **Add up the watts and the daily Wh.** A fridge alone is about 1,440Wh per day at a 60W average.
3. **Check surge.** Motors (fridge, sump pump, well pump) need a high surge rating.
4. **Add solar** if outages could last more than a day.

For many homeowners a **2-4 kWh power station plus panels** is the right first step. A whole-house system makes sense if you need central AC, electric heat or a well pump running at the same time.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a portable power station run my whole house?
Not realistically. The average U.S. home uses about 29 kWh per day, while a typical portable power station stores 1 to 4 kWh. They are meant for critical loads like a fridge, router and lights.

### What is the difference between a power station and a whole-house battery?
A portable power station plugs into appliances with normal cords. A whole-house battery is installed by an electrician and can power most circuits in the home.

### How many kWh does an average home use per day?
About 29 kWh per day, based on the EIA's average of roughly 10,800 kWh per year. Your own usage varies.

### Do I need a transfer switch for a portable power station?
Not if you plug appliances directly into the unit. A transfer switch and a licensed electrician are needed to power home circuits.

### Is a portable power station worth it for outages?
For keeping a fridge, router, lights and medical devices running through short and medium outages, yes. For long outages or whole-home loads, you need more capacity or solar.
