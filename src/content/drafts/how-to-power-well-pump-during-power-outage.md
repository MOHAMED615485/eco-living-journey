## Why Well Pumps Are Different From Every Other Appliance

Every electric motor has two power requirements: running watts and startup surge watts.

For most appliances the difference is manageable. A refrigerator needs 150W running and 600W surge — 4x ratio. Most generators handle that easily.

Well pumps are different. A ½ HP submersible well pump needs approximately 900W running and up to **3,000-4,000W at startup**. A 1 HP pump needs 1,500W running and up to **5,000-6,000W at startup**.

That is a 4-5x ratio between running and surge requirements — and the surge only lasts 2-3 seconds, but if your generator cannot deliver it, the pump simply will not start.

**This is why most people fail at well pump backup power.** They look at the running watts (manageable), buy a generator sized for that, and discover on day one of an outage that the pump will not start.

---

## How to Find Your Pump's Actual Surge Requirement

Every submersible well pump has a data plate on the motor or control box. You need two numbers:

**HP (horsepower):** The size of the motor
**LRA (Locked Rotor Amps):** The amperage drawn at startup

The surge watt formula: **LRA × 230V = surge watts required**

(Most residential well pumps run on 230V, not 120V)

**Common well pump surge requirements:**

| Pump Size | Running Watts | LRA (typical) | Surge Watts Needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| ½ HP | 900W | 13-17A | 3,000-3,900W |
| ¾ HP | 1,200W | 17-22A | 3,900-5,060W |
| 1 HP | 1,500W | 20-28A | 4,600-6,440W |
| 1.5 HP | 2,200W | 28-38A | 6,440-8,740W |
| 2 HP | 3,000W | 38-50A | 8,740-11,500W |

If you have a 1 HP pump and a generator rated at 3,000W surge, your pump will not start. Period.

---

## The Soft Starter Solution

A soft starter is an electronic device that reduces the startup current surge by 50-70% by ramping the motor speed up gradually rather than starting at full power instantly.

For well pumps, a soft starter changes everything:

**Without soft starter:** 1 HP pump needs 5,000-6,000W surge
**With soft starter:** 1 HP pump needs 2,000-2,500W surge

A $150-200 soft starter installed on a 1 HP pump means you can run it from a 3,000W generator instead of needing a 6,000W generator. That is a $500-1,000 difference in generator cost for $150-200 in parts.

**Important:** Soft starters must be installed by a licensed electrician on submersible pump systems. The pump is inside the well casing and the control box is where the soft starter connects.

## The Manual Pump Alternative

For rural properties where power outages are frequent and long-term, a manual hand pump installed alongside the electric pump is worth serious consideration.

A manual pump installed in the same well casing as the submersible pump costs $800-1,500 installed and requires zero electricity. For emergency water access — drinking, cooking, livestock — it provides unlimited water as long as someone can operate the handle.

This is not an either/or with a solar generator. Many rural properties run both: solar generator for convenience and normal use, manual pump as the absolute emergency fallback.

## The Pressure Tank Factor

Most residential well systems include a pressure tank — a sealed tank that stores pressurized water so the pump does not run every time you open a tap.

A properly sized pressure tank holds 20-50 gallons of usable water. When power goes out, that stored water is immediately available without running the pump at all.

**Before you size a solar generator for your well pump, check your pressure tank.** If it holds 40 gallons and your household uses 50 gallons per day, you only need to run the pump once per day to refill it — not continuously. That changes your generator sizing calculation significantly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a solar generator run a well pump?
Yes, but it requires careful sizing based on your pump's surge watt requirement — not just its running watts. A ½ HP well pump needs up to 4,000W at startup. Most solar generators under 2,000W cannot start a well pump without a soft starter device ($150-200) that reduces startup surge by 50-70%.

### What size generator do I need for a well pump?
For a ½ HP pump without a soft starter: minimum 4,800W surge capacity (Bluetti AC200L). For a ½ HP pump with a soft starter: 2,000-3,000W surge is sufficient. For a 1 HP pump without soft starter: 6,000W+ surge required. For a 1 HP pump with soft starter: 3,000-4,000W surge sufficient.

### How do I find my well pump's surge requirement?
Locate the data plate on your pump motor or control box. Find the LRA (Locked Rotor Amps) rating. Multiply LRA × 230 to get the surge watt requirement. Example: LRA of 20A × 230V = 4,600W surge needed.

### What is a soft starter for a well pump?
A soft starter is an electronic device that reduces a motor's startup current surge by ramping the speed up gradually rather than starting at full power. For well pumps it reduces startup surge by 50-70%, allowing a smaller generator to start a larger pump. Professional installation is required for submersible pump applications.

### How long will a solar generator run a well pump?
A ½ HP pump (900W running) on a 2,000Wh battery runs approximately 1.8-2 hours continuously — enough to pump 900-1,400 gallons depending on pump flow rate. Most households can manage daily water needs with one 2-hour pumping session per generator charge.

### Is a hand pump better than a solar generator for a well?
For absolute reliability during extended outages, a manual hand pump is more reliable than any electrical solution. For daily convenience and higher water volume, a solar generator wins. Many rural properties benefit from both — solar generator for normal operation, manual pump as emergency backup.
