## Why Heat Management Matters More Than You Think

Your body starts struggling at indoor temperatures above 80°F. Heat exhaustion begins at prolonged exposure above 90°F. Heat stroke — a life-threatening emergency — can occur in under one hour at temperatures above 104°F indoors.

During a summer power outage in Florida or Texas, an unventilated home can reach 95-100°F indoors within hours. For elderly adults, infants, and people with heart or respiratory conditions, this is a medical emergency.

Know the warning signs:
- Heavy sweating or no sweating (hot, dry skin is more dangerous)
- Dizziness, confusion, or slurred speech
- Nausea or fainting
- Body temperature above 103°F

If any of these appear: cool the person immediately with wet towels on pulse points and seek emergency help.

## 12 Strategies to Keep Cool Without Power

### Strategy 1: Cross-Ventilation (Most Effective Free Method)

Open windows on opposite sides of the house to create airflow. Hot air rises and exits through upper windows. Cool night air enters through lower windows on the shaded side of the house.

**Best practice:** Close all windows and blinds during the day. Open everything at night when outdoor temperature drops below indoor temperature — typically after 9-10PM.

### Strategy 2: Block All Sunlight During the Day

Up to 40% of indoor heat gain comes through windows. Blackout curtains, closed blinds, or even cardboard over south and west-facing windows dramatically reduce indoor temperature.

**Cost:** $0 with existing blinds. $20-40 for blackout curtains.

### Strategy 3: Go Low

Heat rises. The ground floor or basement is significantly cooler than upper floors. Move sleeping areas to the lowest level of the house during heat emergencies.

**Measurement:** Upper floors can be 10-15°F hotter than ground level during a summer outage.

### Strategy 4: Wet Towel Cooling

Wet a towel with cool water and place on pulse points — wrists, neck, ankles, and forehead. The evaporative cooling effect drops perceived body temperature significantly.

**Enhanced version:** Hang wet sheets in doorways. As air moves through them, evaporative cooling reduces air temperature by 5-10°F.

### Strategy 5: Battery-Powered Fans

A quality battery-powered fan draws only 20-50W — dramatically less than AC — and can run for 8-20 hours on a 1,000Wh solar generator.

**Fan + wet towel combination:** Pointing a fan at a wet towel creates an improvised evaporative cooler that can reduce the area temperature by 10-15°F with zero AC.

### Strategy 6: Freeze Water Bottles

Fill water bottles and freeze them before the outage. During the outage, place frozen bottles in front of a battery fan — the air passing over them creates cold airflow. Place bottles wrapped in a cloth against pulse points for direct cooling.

### Strategy 7: Cool Showers

A 5-minute cool shower drops core body temperature by 1-2°F and provides relief for 30-60 minutes. Multiple family members cycling through cool showers is one of the most effective heat management strategies during an extended outage.

### Strategy 8: Identify Your Coolest Room

Before any summer storm, identify the coolest room in your house — typically a basement, interior bathroom, or north-facing room with no direct sun exposure. Move all sleeping activities there during an outage.

### Strategy 9: Minimize Heat Generation

Every appliance you run generates heat. During an outage, eliminate unnecessary heat sources:
- No cooking indoors — use outdoor propane grill
- Minimize phone and laptop use (screens generate heat)
- Turn off any inverter loads you are not actively using

### Strategy 10: Wet the Roof

If safe to do so, wetting the roof surface with a garden hose before an outage (while water pressure is still available) delays heat transfer through the ceiling for several hours.

### Strategy 11: Know Your Community Cooling Centers

Every major city operates cooling centers during heat emergencies — typically libraries, community centers, and malls. Know the nearest location before any storm season. They are free and can be life-saving during extended outages.

### Strategy 12: The Car AC Option

A modern car AC system can cool a vehicle interior to 68°F in minutes. During extreme heat emergencies, spending 2-3 hours in a parked car with AC running provides critical cooling and recharges your phone simultaneously. Keep the car out of direct sun with windows cracked when using this method.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do you stay cool in a house without power?**
The most effective free strategies: cross-ventilate at night, close all blinds during the day, use wet towels on pulse points, stay on the lowest floor, and use battery-powered fans. Identify your local cooling center as a backup option.

**How hot does a house get without AC during a power outage?**
In summer heat, an unventilated house gains approximately 1-2°F per hour with windows closed. Starting at 75°F indoor temperature, a house can reach 90°F+ within 8 hours on a hot summer day. Upper floors heat faster than ground level.

**Can a solar generator run air conditioning?**
Yes — with the right unit. A 5,000 BTU window AC requires at least 1,500W surge capacity to start reliably. The EcoFlow DELTA 3 Plus with X-Boost handles this. Runtime is approximately 2 hours per charge cycle.

**What temperature is dangerous during a power outage?**
Sustained indoor temperatures above 90°F cause heat exhaustion risk. Above 100°F, heat stroke risk increases significantly — especially for elderly adults, infants, and people with cardiovascular conditions. Prioritize cooling measures and evacuation to cooling centers in extreme heat.

**Do fans help cool a house without AC?**
Fans do not reduce air temperature — they create a wind chill effect that makes you feel cooler. Combined with wet towels on pulse points, fans provide meaningful relief up to about 95°F. Above that temperature, fans alone are insufficient and evacuation to a cooled environment should be considered.
